# Self review

Your own review for the cycle. Read by your manager and often used in calibration. Six questions. The useful ones are honest about what went wrong and concrete about what happened next.

## Questions

1. What are my most important accomplishments in the last 12 months, and what impact did they have?
2. What challenges or set-backs did I face, how did I handle them and what did I learn?
3. What skills do I want to improve, and what steps can I take to grow further in my role? Please be as specific as possible.
4. How well have I communicated and collaborated with my team members and other colleagues?
5. What are my priorities and goals in the coming 6-12 months?
6. Is there anything else you would like to add?

## Hints

**Q1.** Evidence is most useful here. Offer the top themes as candidates and ask which ones matter and why. Follow up with impact: who used it, what changed, what it cost the company not to have it. Watch for shared work claimed as solo and solo work undersold; ask directly.

**Q2.** Listen for a real setback, how it was handled, and what changed afterwards. Follow up with "what would you do differently?" and "what did it cost?". Do not let it turn into a humble-brag; ask for the part that actually went wrong.

**Q3.** Listen for a skill, a reason, and a first step. Follow up: "what is the first thing you would do next week?" and "how would your manager notice it had improved?".

**Q4.** Listen for both directions: how they inform others and how they take input. Follow up with a moment of friction and how it was resolved.

**Q5.** Listen for two or three priorities, not ten. Follow up with what would help and what would get in the way. Tie back to Q3 if a skill goal fits.

**Q6.** Often where the honest sentence lives: how they feel about the job, what they need. Follow up gently only if the answer is empty.

## Draft checklist

- Q1 claims should each have an impact sentence. A list of projects without consequences is a flag.
- Q2 must contain something that actually went wrong. If it does not, say so.
- Q3 and Q5 must contain concrete steps. "Get better at X" without a first step is a flag.
- The review should not contradict itself between Q2 and Q4, or Q3 and Q5.
- Self-criticism should be neither performative nor absent.
