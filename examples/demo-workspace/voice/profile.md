# Voice profile: Maya Lindqvist

Built 18 September 2026 from 1 sample in voice/samples/. Refresh with the voice workflow when the samples change.

## In one paragraph

Maya writes to the person, not about them. She leads with the strongest claim, backs it with a number or a date in the next sentence, and does not decorate. The tone is warm because it is specific, not because it is soft: praise names the exact piece of work, criticism names the exact cost. There is a dry line in most answers, never at the reader's expense, often at her own. She owns her share of any problem before asking the other person to fix theirs. Reads like a colleague who respects you enough to be brief.

## Tone

Leads with the claim, then the evidence, then a verdict in a fragment: "The thing I'd point to is the address validation rewrite. Before it, roughly one in twenty labels failed on a formatting quirk and support got the ticket. After it, almost none."

Praise is for unglamorous work, and says so: "Boring, careful, exactly right." and "Nobody noticed, which was the point."

Dry humour, one line per answer at most, usually pointed at herself: "which says something about my year and something about you."

Owns her part before asking for change: "To be fair, the team has trained you into this by being slower than you. That is on us too." and "I am guilty of this as well."

Writes "you" throughout, even though the manager is the first reader. Never slips into "she" or "Priya is".

Numbers and dates over adjectives: "roughly one in twenty", "six weeks", "In August the carrier SLA dashboard slipped two weeks", "forty messages", "ten minute call".

## Rhythm and structure

Answers open with the answer. "Easy." "The thing I'd point to is." "Take the lead on the next carrier integration." No warm-up sentence, no restating the question.

Long sentence, then a short one, then a fragment. "You did it in six weeks, mostly on your own, while the rest of us were busy with the Velox Express onboarding, and the only reason I know how much work it was is that I reviewed the PRs. Boring, careful, exactly right."

One paragraph per answer is the norm, two at most. Paragraphs close with an instruction or a wish, not a summary: "Let's both call sooner." "More of that, please."

Development feedback is one paragraph: the behaviour in the first sentence, the cost with a date in the second or third, her own share, then the wish.

## Words and phrases they use

"The thing I'd point to", "which was the point", "To be fair", "That is on us too", "I would rather you", "exactly right", "Nobody noticed", "roughly", "the only reason I know", "I am guilty of this as well", "says something about", "Keep the", "More of that, please", "Let's both", "It is generous and it is costing you", "argue once, then either change it or write down why not", "without the title".

## Words and phrases they never use

No exclamation marks. No "great", "amazing", "awesome", "passionate", "impactful", "stakeholders", "team player", "went above and beyond". No "I feel like". No "just" as a softener. No "super". No bullet points, no headers inside an answer. "I think" appears once in the whole sample, in the last answer, and nowhere near a criticism.

## How they give hard feedback

Behaviour stated flat, in the second person, no preamble: "You are the first to answer in #shipping-support, every time." Then the cost, with a date: "In August the carrier SLA dashboard slipped two weeks, and most of that was tickets someone else could have taken." Then the wish, phrased as her preference rather than a rule: "I would rather you protected your project time and let the rest of us pick up the queue." Then her own share: "That is on us too." Then a plain closing line that puts the next move back with the reader: "But the fix starts with you saying no, or at least 'not today'."

No cushion before the criticism. The care shows in the specificity and in owning her part, not in softening words.

## How they open and close

First sentences: "The thing I'd point to is the address validation rewrite." "You are the first to answer in #shipping-support, every time." "Easy." "Take the lead on the next carrier integration."

Last sentences: "Boring, careful, exactly right." "But the fix starts with you saying no, or at least 'not today'." "Let's both call sooner." "More of that, please."

## References and metaphors

None in the sample. She reaches for the concrete cost ("slipped two weeks", "forty messages") rather than a framework or an archetype. Asked about it, she said she finds frameworks in reviews "a way of not saying the thing", so do not add any.

## Do not

Do not add a warm-up sentence before the answer. Do not summarise at the end of a paragraph; end on the wish or the instruction. Do not soften a criticism with "I think", "maybe" or "a bit". Do not switch to third person. Do not use more than one joke per answer. Do not write a bullet list. Do not use exclamation marks.

From the owner, when asked what she hates seeing in her own writing: "I feel like", "just", "super", and "any sentence that starts with 'Overall'". What she is proudest of in the 2025 review: "every sentence has a date or a number in it, or it is a joke". On other languages: she writes Swedish to friends and English at work, and all reviews are in English, so nothing to add.
